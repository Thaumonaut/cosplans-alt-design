## Persona

**Name / Archetype:** Michael — The Regular

**Age:** Mid-20s

**Relationship to product:** New user — first session after signup

**Cosplay context:** Long-time hobbyist; attends several cons and local events each year. Comfortable with planning basics but still refining how he juggles projects and deadlines.

**Tech comfort:** Medium — uses specific apps heavily (messaging, social, notes), open to new tools if they clearly help him stay organized.

**Persona ref:** PERS-001

**Product:** Cosplans

## Assumptions
<!-- Conditions this journey depends on that are stated as given, not validated -->
- Michael has Instagram installed on the same phone he uses for the PWA and is comfortable using share to send content to other apps.
- He is on Android; the Cosplans PWA is not installed at the start but he knows how to use a PWA and installs it during the journey.
- He has good internet so changes sync quickly or near-instantly between desktop and phone.
- He is willing to sign in on the PWA when prompted and does not drop off at that step.

## Trigger

Michael was already looking for a planning tool. A new cosplay idea — a character from an anime he watched with his roommate — gave him a concrete reason to sign up for Cosplans today after hearing about it from a cosplay friend.

## Journey

### 1. Sign up and create an idea

Michael signs up for Cosplans and creates a new idea. He uses character search, which returns an image and relevant information for the character he wants to cosplay. He selects the character; the idea is auto-named after the character and series. He renames it to a custom name he prefers and is happy with the idea’s setup.

### 2. Open the moodboard and get oriented

He opens the moodboard tab and sees a get-started prompt. He has an Instagram reel in mind that fits this character. He opens his phone and installs the Cosplans PWA on his Android device, then opens Instagram, finds the reel, and shares it to Cosplans.

### 3. Capture from mobile

The Cosplans PWA opens and prompts him to sign in. After signing in, he adds the reel to the idea he just created. He returns to the website and sees the Instagram reel on the moodboard — a key moment that reinforces that the flow works and keeps him going.

### 4. Build and connect on the board

He adds a note node with his ideas for how to use the reel later. The moodboard surfaces a hint that he can link the note and the reel with an edge so the note is visible when reviewing the reel. He does that, then continues building out the board with fabric options, patterns, and reference images. He feels excited and then satisfied as the board takes shape.

### 5. Peak — everything connected

Seeing his ideas connected and organized in one place, with a variety of node types and edges keeping related items together, gives him pride and relief. He feels the board will actually help him later when he works on the project.

## Key Emotional Moment

The emotional peak is when the moodboard is built out and everything feels connected and organized — pride and relief that his ideas have a real home and will support him later. An important earlier moment is the reel appearing on the board after sharing from his phone (“it actually worked”), which builds trust and keeps him going.

## Success Signal

Michael closes the tab feeling good and plans to come back before his next con to turn this idea into a project.

## Failure Path

If Share Target fails, the PWA doesn’t open, or he gets stuck signing in on mobile, he falls back to adding the reel manually on desktop (e.g. pasting the URL). The journey still completes with more friction.

## Follow-on journeys

- Come back before con to turn this idea into a project (idea → project conversion, tasks, deadline).
- Continue adding to this moodboard over time (more nodes, edges, references) before converting to a project.

## User Research Questions

**Screener** *(who to recruit)*
- Long-time cosplay hobbyist who attends at least one con per year
- Uses Instagram and/or TikTok for cosplay inspiration
- Has looked for or tried planning/organization tools for cosplay (notes, boards, spreadsheets)

**Discovery interviews** *(open-ended, validate the problem space)*
- When you get a new cosplay idea, what do you do first? Where do you put it?
- Have you ever shared something from Instagram or TikTok into another app to save it? What worked or frustrated you?
- What would make you trust a new tool enough to sign in on your phone when it opens from a share?

**Usability / concept testing** *(validate specific product moments)*
- Task: Create an idea, use character search, then add one item to the moodboard from your phone via share. Where do you get stuck or hesitate?
- After they link two nodes with an edge: How does that change how you’d use this board later?

**Post-launch validation** *(confirm the success signal is measurable)*
- % of new users who create an idea and add at least one moodboard item in the first session
- Follow-up: For users who added a moodboard item in session one, did they return before a con (or within 30 days) to convert the idea to a project or add more items?
